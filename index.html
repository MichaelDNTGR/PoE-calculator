Good to know

PoE classes and power
Class Standard Switch reserves (PSE) Device receives (PD) PSE voltage PD voltage

The difference between PSE and PD power is lost in the cable (up to 100 m). Switches budget on the PSE value.

Static and dynamic power management
Dynamic mode (default)
The switch deducts the power each port actually draws. Unused power can be shared with other devices. Real-world consumption is usually lower than the class maximum, so this calculator's class-based totals are a safe upper bound.
Static mode
The switch reserves the maximum power for each port, based on the class the device announces or a power limit you configure. Reserved power is never shared. Use the class wattage in this calculator to match static mode.
Port priority
If the budget runs out, for example after a power supply failure, the switch powers down the lowest priority ports first. Set critical devices to high or critical priority.
Redundant (RPS) and shared (EPS) power
Redundant budget
The PoE budget that remains when any single power supply fails. If your load is below this value, every device stays powered after a failure.
EPS (shared) mode
Power supplies work together for the highest PoE budget. If one fails, the budget drops and low-priority ports may lose power.
RPS (redundant) mode
The second power supply is a hot standby. The budget stays the same after a failure.
Data sources and disclaimer

All PoE budgets come from the official NETGEAR M4250, M4300 and M4350 datasheets. Combinations that are not published in a datasheet, such as two different modular PSU models in one switch, are not offered. Always confirm your design with the current datasheet and your NETGEAR contact before ordering.
